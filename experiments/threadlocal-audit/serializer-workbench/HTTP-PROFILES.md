## Interpretation

The typed path preserves packed objects and creates a fresh generator; it changes dispatch only. Expected source-level effect is bypassing generic String/ObjectArray/IndexedList serializers and ObjectMapper configuration for compact row payloads, while JSON state checks, escaping, packed-term construction, buffering and record flushes remain. Disappearing generic frames therefore do not mean all serialization overhead disappeared.

Clean primary completion latency improved 8.11% on depth, but serializer-alone and combined-versus-base superiority remain inconclusive and first-row latency worsened in the serializer-alone comparison. Profiles cannot explain that first-row tradeoff causally or convert the inconclusive interaction into proven synergy. Keep the independently measured mixed outcome.

Sampled allocation class weights are highly variable, include setup/metrics, and are neither exact allocated bytes nor retained heap. No GC or generator-allocation saving is established by these profiles. Any generator reuse or elimination of packed term objects would be a separate correctness-sensitive candidate, not part of the tested patch. No additional clean round or automatic candidate adoption follows.

## Observed dispatch shift

NDJSON-nearest samples were 153/1001 (base), 128/898 (depth), 82/866 (serializer), and 81/829 (both). The verifyValueWrite leaf had 114/95/14/14 samples. Base/depth top stacks use generic StringSerializer, ObjectArraySerializer and IndexedListSerializer; serializer/both top stacks instead use WorkbenchPageResultWriter.writePackedValue/writePackedRow, with String.charAt/string escaping visible. This verifies the intended actual-path dispatch change. It does not imply value-state checks were removed: inlining and changed sampling denominators can alter leaf visibility. The sampled contrast supports a mechanism, not an exact attribution of the clean latency gain.
